The short answer: **Spain's Pablo Hernández de Cos will most likely be the next ECB president, probably appointed at the EU summit on 17–18 December.** But this is not a simple two-way race. France and Germany are dividing up three seats.

## 1. Why the sudden change

On 29 October Lagarde announced she would step down on **7 February 2027**, about nine months before her eight-year term ends in late October 2027. Her reason was blunt: central bankers must stay neutral, and she "cannot remain neutral"; she wants "freedom of expression". Many link this to France's presidential election next spring; she says she is "not a candidate for anything".

Two more board members are leaving too:

- **Isabel Schnabel (Germany)**, in charge of market operations, leaves on 3 January for the IMF;
- **Philip Lane (Ireland)**, chief economist, whose term ends at the end of May.

Three of six board members gone in five months. Eurogroup president Kyriakos Pierrakakis has said the three posts will probably be settled as a package.

## 2. The two candidates

Nominations closed on 6 November with two names:

- **Hernández de Cos (Spain)**: Bank of Spain governor 2018–2024, BIS General Manager since July 2025. Moderate, a consensus builder. He would be the first Spaniard to lead the ECB.
- **Klaas Knot (Netherlands)**: Dutch central bank president 2011–2025, long a hawkish voice on the Governing Council.

Both are central-banking veterans. The difference is orientation: de Cos is friendlier to fiscally stretched southern Europe; Knot puts more weight on inflation control.

## 3. The real table: a Franco-German deal

According to El País and Handelsblatt, the deal under discussion is:

- **President: de Cos**, backed by France;
- **Chief economist: Germany**, with Merz's office favouring Princeton's Markus Brunnermeier, which would make him the first German chief economist since Jürgen Stark left in 2011;
- **Schnabel's market-operations seat: France**.

If Knot won instead, France would get chief economist and Germany would keep market operations. Either way, **both deputy seats probably go to France and Germany**.

Merz said in Dublin on 13 November that a deal was "in sight but not final". Nominations for the other two posts close on **18 November**; António Costa aims to start consulting Parliament and the Governing Council at the **26 November** summit; the target for a final decision is **17–18 December**.

## 4. The euro area the new president inherits

- **Inflation**: **4.0%** in October (flash), twice target, driven by Middle East energy prices; 2.3% excluding energy.
- **Rates**: deposit rate **2.5%**, held on 29 October. A Reuters poll on 12 November found most economists expect **another hike on 17 December**; Bundesbank chief Joachim Nagel said on 13 November that further action is "highly probable" if conditions persist.
- **Growth**: Q3 GDP **+0.4%** q/q, but about 0.2 points came from Ireland (+5.5%). Excluding Ireland, 0.2%; France **−0.1%**, Italy **0**, Spain **+0.6%**. Employment flat.

The new president will likely face one question first: **when to stop hiking**. Markets would probably read de Cos as a signal of stopping sooner.
